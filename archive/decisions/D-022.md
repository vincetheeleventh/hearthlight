# D-022 · Workflows are first-class, catalogued, and compared on evidence

**Date:** 2026-08-05 · **Source:** Vince, this session · **Agent:** cowork

**Context:** Hearthlight's pipeline was linear — one route from storyboard to clip, via a conditioning
still per shot. Vince is now running a second, genuinely different route in parallel: storyboard
straight to video, conditioned on character sheets, environment sheets and a style image rather than
on an approved frame. He has already had success with a low-effort variant — handing a video model an
*image of the storyboard spreadsheet* and telling it to create what the board depicts.

These are not stages of one another. They are alternatives that fail differently, and he wants to run
both, find where each wins, and keep the answer.

**Reason:** A linear pipeline cannot hold two routes. Left undocumented, the second route lives in
whichever chat window it was tried in, and the comparison produces opinions rather than an answer —
exactly the failure `GOALS.md` core problem 4 names: *workflows live in one person's head.*

The catalog also makes the comparison honest. Without a ledger, "which one is better" is settled by
whichever was tried most recently. With one, it is settled by attempts-to-approval per shot type, and
the losing route is **parked with its evidence**, not deleted — a route that loses on dialogue may
still win on action.

**Consequences:**

- **`workflows/`** is a canon directory. `README.md` is the index, the selection guide, and the
  comparison ledger. One document per route: what it is, when to use it, inputs, steps, cost, known
  failure modes, status.
- **WF-A (Shot-Image → Video)** is documented as the active v1 path.
- **WF-B (Storyboard → Video Direct)** is documented as an active parallel trial, with two variants:
  **B1** board-image plus plain instruction (proven, low effort, low control) and **B2** per-shot
  structured prompt (full control, no still).
- **A workflow with no named failure modes is not catalogued yet.** Stating where a route *should*
  win, before the trial, is what lets the trial falsify it.
- **`hearthlight-video-prompts` now branches on workflow.** A WF-A prompt carries motion only,
  because the still carries the look; a WF-B prompt must also carry blocking, optics, geography and
  constraints. Writing one for the other route is the common error.
- **The trial ends when the ledger says something**, not when someone feels sure. The winner is
  promoted, the loser parked, the lesson recorded here, and `PRODUCT_SPEC.md` updated to describe
  what is actually run.

**Two skills were adapted from an outside production practice** to support this, with
platform-specific and proprietary references removed:

- **`hearthlight-acting`** — performance writing. Cross-cutting; both routes need it. Behaviour
  instead of emotion, muscle instead of adjectives, a locked master profile adapted per scene, and
  the eye-life rules. Its central claim is that emotion words make a model improvise shallow, while a
  goal plus an obstacle plus changing tactics produces emotion as a by-product.
- **`hearthlight-video-prompts/references/prompt-architecture.md`** — the structured skeleton, the
  `GEO SPATIAL LAYOUT` block, optics by diagonal field of view rather than millimetres, positive-form
  constraints, and the iteration discipline.

**The asset discipline that WF-B depends on is not yet in Hearthlight's skills** — state-splitting
(`@char`, `@char_wet` as separate assets), 3/4 location sheets with a physical anchor, the
deliberately plain character sheet with no film look baked in, and the ten-out-of-ten stress test.
These change existing skill contracts, so they are AMBER: filed in `PROPOSALS.md`, not applied.
WF-B's document says plainly that the current sheets do not meet the standard it needs.
