# D-017 · Canon states the present; the archive remembers

**Date:** 2026-08-04 · **Source:** Vince, this session · **Agent:** cowork

**Context:** Within two days of the canon being written, `ROADMAP.md` had accumulated struck-through
questions with dated answers beside them, "known problems" annotated *(Resolved 2026-08-03)*, and
reclassification notes explaining what a row used to say. `DECISIONS.md` had grown to 316 lines,
almost all of it argument rather than rule. Every agent read all of it at the start of every
session. Vince: *"These documents themselves are not records of past decisions. They are currently
the highest priority signal rich information on the current state."*

**Reason:** Two different jobs were being done by one file, and the cheaper one was crowding out the
expensive one. Current state is read constantly and must be short; history is read almost never and
can be arbitrarily long. Merging them makes every session pay the cost of history it will not use,
and — worse — makes the current state harder to *see*, because a resolved item and a live one look
alike on the page.

Deleting the history outright was rejected. In a repo where three agents share no memory, the
argument behind a rule is the only thing stopping the next agent from quietly reversing it. The
insurance is real; paying its premium on every read is not.

So the two halves separate. A decision keeps its **law** in canon (one line, in force, binds
something) and sends its **argument** to the archive. A canon doc keeps what is true now and sends
everything else to `archive/{doc}.md`, dated, with one line on why it left.

YAML front matter was added at the same time and for the same reason: an agent should be able to
route to the right document by reading ~10 lines of metadata rather than skimming nine bodies. The
`answers:` list carries the questions a doc is the right place to ask; `not_here:` routes the
questions it deliberately does not answer.

**Consequences:** `governance/CANON-RULES.md` holds the law and the front-matter schema.
`governance/canon.py check` validates front matter and scans for history patterns — strikethrough,
resolved-stamps, answered-stamps, reclassification notes, doc-revision narration — and runs inside
`checkpoint.py commit`, blocking on violation like the secret scan. `archive/` is append-only and
not read by default. `DECISIONS.md` becomes a table of laws; `archive/decisions/D-0XX.md` holds one
file per argument, each carrying `in_force:` and `superseded_by:`. Superseding a decision now means
flipping those fields and replacing one table row, rather than appending a contradiction and hoping
a reader notices the order.

`GOALS.md` receives front matter but its body stays untouched — it is `owner: vince`, and the
archive rule does not grant an agent write access to it.
